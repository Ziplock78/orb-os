Preamble

The Orb is an instrument, not an application

It sits on a desk inside peripheral vision and is looked at far more often than it is used. The typical interaction is a glance of under two seconds from across a room, and the question behind that glance is almost never "what is that aircraft." It is "is anything happening, and is my instrument alive."

Everything below follows from one finding that cost us two weeks and one visibly broken device to learn properly:

An instrument earns trust through rhythm, and loses it through hesitation. The finding this charter is built on

Our users do not measure frame rate. They measure whether the thing looks like it is working. Those are different quantities, they are optimised by different decisions, and for two weeks we optimised the wrong one.

This document is binding. Every software requirement for the Flight Radar, in firmware and in Orb Studio, must trace to a principle here. A requirement that traces to nothing is out of scope. A principle a requirement cannot satisfy is an escalation, not a negotiation. Nothing in this charter is aspirational. Every line was paid for, and the receipts are in Part VI.

Part I

The glance contract

Three commitments that define the surface everything else is designed onto.

  • Legible in under two seconds, from two metres, in peripheral vision. If a state needs to be read to be understood, it is designed for the wrong distance.
  • Never demands attention it cannot repay. An ambient object that interrupts gets turned around to face the wall. Interruption is the most expensive act available to us and is spent at most once.
  • Trustworthy without interrogation. The moment a user has to check whether the dial is current, the instrument has already failed at its only job.

Part II

Experience principles

Nine principles. Each states what must be true, why humans read it that way, what it forbids, and the evidence that settles a dispute about it.

P1

Evenness before rate

The sweep's frame to frame interval must be even before it is fast. Where the two conflict, evenness wins, every time.

Why
Human motion perception is tuned far more finely to constant velocity than to temporal resolution. Nobody can tell 8 frames per second from 12. Everybody reads a step that arrives late as the mechanism straining, and a straining mechanism is one that is about to fail. Identical nominal frame rates, delivered evenly and unevenly, produce opposite judgements of whether the product is any good.
Forbids
Treating frames per second as a success metric. Deriving the sweep's angular step from a raw frame time rather than a smoothed one. Any per frame cost that varies with the sweep's own angle.
Proven by
Interval spread across a rolling window, never the average. See T1.
P2

The instrument never blames itself for the world

When data stops arriving, the dial must distinguish "I am broken" from "the sky is quiet" from "the source is not answering," and must never assert a cause it has not directly observed.

Why
People attribute failure to the nearest visible agent. An Orb that goes blank because a third party feed started refusing requests is, to its owner, a broken Orb. A confident wrong explanation is worse than none: it spends trust on a claim the user can disprove in ten seconds with the phone in their hand.
Forbids
Any status text asserting a cause that is not directly measured. Radio association is not internet reachability. Reachability is not service health. We have shipped a banner that said the network was fine while the device could not reach anything.
Proven by
Every user visible status string maps to one observed fact, enumerated in the status ledger. A string with no fact behind it does not ship.
P3

Silence is a claim

A dial showing old contacts without saying they are old is lying. Staleness must be expressed in the instrument's own visual language before it is ever expressed in words.

Why
An ambient display's whole value is being trustable at a glance. But text is the most expensive element on a glanceable surface, because reading is not glancing. Dimming a contact costs nothing and says the same thing.
Forbids
Rendering contacts past the staleness threshold at full fidelity. Using words as the first expression of degradation.
Proven by
The state table in Part III, which defines the dial's appearance in every world state including the ones we would rather not think about.
P4

One voice, never an alarm

At most one advisory may be present at a time. It is the lowest element in the visual hierarchy. It is never modal, never animated, never repeated, and never covers the dial.

Why
Alarm fatigue is the best documented failure mode in clinical monitoring: a device that warns often teaches its users to stop reading warnings, including the one that mattered. On a desk object the penalty arrives faster, because the user can simply stop looking at it.
Forbids
Modal errors. Toasts. Blinking. More than one advisory. Any advisory at all for a condition the user cannot act on.
Proven by
Advisory audit: count and hierarchy position recorded for every state in Part III.
P5

An empty sky is a valid reading

Zero aircraft in range must look exactly like a healthy instrument, because that is what it is.

Why
A user who set a 30 km range and a five contact cap will, at three in the morning, see nothing. If nothing looks like failure, we have taught them that our instrument fails every night. The sweep turning over a correctly dressed empty dial is the entire signal: the machine is alive and the sky is quiet.
Forbids
Any "no data" messaging triggered by an empty but healthy result. Any visual difference between "no aircraft" and "no aircraft yet."
Proven by
State S5, captured and compared against S6.
P6

The tool cannot promise what the device cannot deliver

Orb Studio's preview is a contract. Anything it renders, the Orb renders. Where the device cannot, Studio refuses the design. It does not warn and proceed.

Why
A preview that overstates the device turns every install into a small disappointment, and teaches the user to distrust the preview, which was the only reason to open the tool. Refusal is honest and instantly understood. A warning that is followed by a worse result is neither.
Forbids
Warn and continue on a capability mismatch. Previewing at a fidelity, size, or smoothness the device cannot reach.
Proven by
Capability level checked at install, and preview parity captures rendered from the same theme through both paths.
P7

A design choice carries its cost at authoring time

Where a decision costs smoothness, Studio says so while the user is making it, in the units the user cares about, not ours.

Why
This is the principle we learned most expensively. A sweep hand was authored on a 489 by 489 canvas whose visible ink was 86 by 272: ninety seven per cent of what the Orb rotated ten times a second was empty. Nothing in the tool mentioned it. The user experienced it as choppy firmware, and we spent days inside the firmware. The user made a reasonable choice with no information, so the failure was ours.
Forbids
Shipping an authored asset without first normalising it to its true cost. Accepting a design the tool can predict will stutter without saying anything.
Proven by
The bake trims every rotating sprite to its ink and carries the pivot with it, asserted by test. The panel reports a smoothness estimate for the sweep.
P8

What the design says, the device does exactly

Range, contact cap, layer order and every other authored value are promises. The device honours them literally or refuses the theme.

Why
The user's entire mental model is "it shows me what I told it to show." A cap quietly exceeded or a layer quietly reordered breaks the only model they have, and it breaks it invisibly, which means they lose confidence without ever learning why. We shipped a theme whose info card sat above the aircraft in Studio and below them on the device: one theme, two different truths.
Forbids
Silently clamping an authored value. Any device side reordering that cannot be expressed in Studio.
Proven by
Authored value compared against the device's own reported state, per theme.
P9

Nothing on the dial is invented

Every contact is a real aircraft. Every coastline, road and airport is real geography. When we have nothing, we show nothing and say so. We never show something plausible.

Why
The instrument's claim is that it is a window onto the actual sky. One fabricated contact, discovered once, retroactively invalidates every reading the user has ever taken from it. There is no recovering from that, and no amount of polish elsewhere compensates.
Forbids
Demo data in shipping firmware. Interpolated or predicted contacts presented as observed. Decorative map geometry presented as real. Dependence on a single feed provider, which is how a whole category of aircraft quietly disappears.
Proven by
Declared source list with explicit precedence and failover, and a shipping build that contains no synthetic contact path.

Part III

What the dial looks like in every world state

The states are not error handling. They are the product. Most of a user's hours with the Orb are spent in states 4 and 5, so those are designed first, not last.

IDWorld stateSweepContactsWords on screen
S1 Booting, no position fix Held still, dial fully dressed None One determinate line naming the step, never an open ended "loading"
S2 No network Turning normally Aging, dimming on schedule One advisory, lowest hierarchy, naming what is missing and not why
S3 Network up, source refusing Turning normally Aging, dimming on schedule Same treatment as S2. We do not diagnose what we cannot observe
S4 Live, contacts present Turning normally Full fidelity, capped as authored None
S5 Live, empty sky Turning normally None None. Identical to S4 minus the contacts
S6 Stale beyond threshold Turning normally Outlined rather than filled One advisory, only past T4
S7 Installing a theme Suspended None Determinate progress. Every step over two seconds is accounted for on screen

Part IV

Timing budgets

These are acceptance thresholds, not targets. A build that misses one does not ship pending a written exception recorded against the principle it violates.

IDBudgetThresholdEnforces

T2 Sweep mean interval ≤ 120 ms P1
T3 Power on to first real contact drawn ≤ 25 s P5
T4 Contacts dim / outline / earn an advisory 60 s / 120 s / 180 s P3, P4
T5 Selection card shows identity from cache ≤ 400 ms P8
T6 Selection card persists after its last field arrives, and never dismisses while a lookup it started is still in flight ≥ 5 s P8

Part V

Engineering covenants

Part II binds the product. This part binds us. Every covenant below exists because we broke it once and the user paid for it, so each one carries its receipt.

C1

Instrument the scarce resource, in its own units

For every constrained resource there must be a diagnostic reporting that resource. A diagnostic reporting its abundant sibling is worse than none, because it manufactures confidence.

Paid for: every memory diagnostic printed PSRAM, megabytes free, while the pool that actually ran out was internal RAM at 7 KB. Two days went into fixing real but irrelevant problems downstream of it. Adding one line that printed the right number answered the whole question on the next boot.

C2

Measure the distribution, never the average

Any metric describing something a human perceives over time reports minimum, mean, maximum and spread. An average alone is not admissible evidence.

Paid for: a 263 ms mean concealed a 240 to 458 ms swing, and the swing was the entire complaint. The average was stable while the product was visibly deteriorating.

C3

No hidden authority

Any cache, bake or overlay that shadows a source must announce which source served each asset, expose its fingerprint, and provide an explicit invalidation path.

Paid for: themes are baked into a flash partition, and the asset loader reads flash before the SD card. Files pushed to the card were silently ignored while the log said "already baked and unchanged." A performance improvement was measured, believed and reported against an artefact that was never loaded.

C4

Test off the default value

Every parameter with a default gets at least one test at a non default value. Every transform gets at least one capture away from identity.

Paid for: the sweep pivot was only ever exercised at the sprite's centre, which is also the graphics library's default. A pivot that never applied would have looked perfect. Every screenshot anyone had taken was at zero degrees, the one angle at which the library skips the rotation entirely.

C5

Assert at the boundary the device reads

Tests run against the artefact the device actually consumes, the shipped bytes, not against the intermediate the code happened to produce.

Paid for: a preview drew the sweep art into a dial sized box while the bake shipped it at natural size. Both were self consistent. Neither matched the other.

C6

An improvement is not real until the changed artefact is proven to be the one that ran

Before reporting a gain, fingerprint what executed. If the fingerprint is unchanged, the measurement is void regardless of how good the number looks.

Paid for: a 1.7x improvement was measured and reported. It was an artefact of the hand being clipped off the edge of the screen by a mismatched pivot. The change being credited had never loaded.

C7

Say the action first

Anything the user must do goes at the top, in one block, before any explanation. A retraction goes in the same place, never later in the same paragraph.

Paid for: an instruction was given, then revised further down the same message. The user acted on the first thing they read and lost the work. Prose order is interface design.

Part VI

Incident ledger

The evidence base. Each principle above is the generalisation of a specific thing that went wrong on a real device in front of a real user.

What the user sawWhat was actually trueNow governed by
"The sweep is choppy" Frame cost tracked the rotated bounding box of a square sprite, which grows twofold between 0 and 45 degrees. The hand sped up and slowed down once per revolution P1, P7
"Stuck on loading aircraft and location data" A range change triggered a full reprojection of coastlines, roads and airports on the render thread, freezing it for 2.5 seconds P2, T7
"Why does my Orb say no WiFi" The banner asserted a cause from radio association state, which reports nothing about whether anything is reachable P2
"Why would a user use this if warnings keep popping up" Correct. Every warning was individually defensible and collectively fatal P4
"The info card is under the aircraft on the Orb and on top in Studio" Layer order was expressed twice, in two places, and the two disagreed P8
"The airport info does not arrive before the card disappears" The dismissal timer was independent of the lookups the selection had started T5, T6
"The hand rotates around the dial instead of its fulcrum" A new pivot was applied to an old sprite, because the flash bake shadowed the card and nothing said so C3, C6
"Aircraft stopped arriving" The network stack had roughly 7 KB of working memory. Every fix upstream of that was real and none of them held C1

Part VII

Amendment

This charter changes by evidence, not by opinion or by convenience. A principle is amended when an observation contradicts it, and the amendment records the observation that forced it. A principle is never relaxed because a requirement is hard to meet: that case is an exception, written down, dated, and attached to the principle it violates, so the debt is visible rather than absorbed.

Anyone may propose an amendment. The evidence standard is the same for everyone, and it is the standard in Part V: measured at the boundary the device reads, with the artefact fingerprinted, reporting distribution rather than average.
